Research

An intelligent, quantitative
science of structure.

The properties of matter arise from its atomic and electronic structure, yet even two grains of the same compound can differ in consequential ways. We develop an AI-enhanced crystallography platform that resolves structural and electronic differences with precision, reproducibility, and scale, transforming electron diffraction into a routine, quantitative method that connects structure to function across chemistry, materials science, and medicine.

01

Three integrated thrusts

Together these thrusts build a closed, intelligent electron-crystallography loop, where machine learning improves data accuracy, multimodal analysis adds chemical context, and autonomous refinement feeds results back to guide new data collection.

Thrust I

Machine learning for accurate diffraction

MicroED enables atomic-resolution structure determination from nanocrystals, but the accuracy of unit-cell measurement is its weakest point. By integrating powder X-ray diffraction with microED through machine learning, we correct systematic errors, reconcile nanoscale and bulk data, and turn electron diffraction into a quantitatively reliable method. This underpins polymorph discrimination, disordered-framework analysis, and reproducibility across laboratories.

Thrust II

Multimodal Diffraction-Imaging Mass Spectrometry

In real systems, crystallization rarely yields a single pure phase; co-crystals, polymorphs, and trace impurities are the norm. We couple electron diffraction with imaging mass spectrometry and integrate both data streams with domain-tuned models to assign atomic structure and chemical identity crystal by crystal. This lets us quantify purity and polymorph distributions directly from complex reaction mixtures, redefining small-molecule crystallization as a data problem.

Thrust III

AI-driven structure refinement

Despite decades of progress, structure refinement remains a manual, interpretive process across both X-ray and electron diffraction. By embedding graph neural networks and adaptive AI agents into refinement, we close the loop between data acquisition and structure elucidation, refining hundreds of nanocrystal structures reproducibly and unifying X-ray and electron crystallography under a single autonomous framework.

02

Building what we study

Sustainable synthesis

Our group both synthesizes novel molecular and crystalline materials and builds the autonomous tools to characterize them. We develop sustainable synthetic methods that leverage non-covalent interactions at aqueous interfaces for the efficient synthesis of building blocks and added-value chemicals. New materials challenge and advance our analytical tools, while structural feedback from AI-driven characterization guides the design of the next generation of molecules and materials.

Both directions share common infrastructure in mass spectrometry, electron microscopy, and computational resources including the Texas Advanced Computing Center (TACC).
